Isomerism in coordination compounds is divided into Structural Isomerism and Stereoisomerism.

**Structural Isomerism:**
- **Ionisation:** Exchange of coordinated ligand with ionisable counter ion.
- **Hydrate:** Water molecules swap between inner coordination sphere and outer crystal lattice.
- **Linkage:** Arises in complexes with ambidentate ligands (e.g., $NO_2^-$ vs $ONO^-$).
- **Coordination:** Interchange of ligands between complex cation and complex anion.

**Stereoisomerism:**
- **Geometrical:** *cis* (adjacent) vs *trans* (opposite) in square planar and octahedral complexes. *fac* (facial) vs *mer* (meridional) in octahedral $[Ma_3b_3]$.
- **Optical:** Non-superimposable mirror images (chiral enantiomers). Complex must lack a plane of symmetry. e.g., $cis-[Co(en)_2Cl_2]^+$ is optically active, while $trans-[Co(en)_2Cl_2]^+$ is optically inactive.
